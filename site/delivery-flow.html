How work ships: the delivery flow

This is the habit that turns agent output into finished work: every change — every single one — flows through the same five-stage pipe before it lands.

  1. Spec. Write down the change first: the problem, the evidence, the proposed fix, the files touched, and how it will be tested. If the writing is hard, the change isn't understood yet.
  2. Plan. Break the spec into ordered steps small enough to finish and check one at a time.
  3. Build. Execute the steps, committing each one. Tests run from the first step, not at the end.
  4. Independent review. A reviewer who didn't write the change reads it against the spec and reports findings. The author fixes and re-runs the checks. No self-approval, ever.
  5. Merge. When checks pass and review converges, the work lands — and the human owner gives the final go.

Proof, not promises: you're looking at a product of this exact flow. This tutorial exists as a written spec and plan, was checked against the real documentation, went through independent review, and ships only after its checks pass. The pattern works because the agents must show their receipts.

One brain serves one person well. When a whole team needs to share it, that's the last step.
